# Core watchlist adaptive-breakout audit

Review every stock currently returned by the `core` watchlist over roughly the latest three to six months. This is a causal case-study audit, not a profitability backtest: compare the engine's day-by-day floor, peak, active ceiling, major ceiling, tests, failed attempts, and breakout labels with the price sequence visible at that date.

The audit must actively search for stale active-ceiling ATR, the moving-goalpost defect where rejected higher wicks repeatedly raise the line, missed nearer ranges, premature ceiling replacement, false or late breakouts, and misleading state explanations. Preserve the separation between structural detection and Buy Review quality. Do not change code yet; finish with evidence per stock, recurring failure modes, counterexamples where the present rule works, and the smallest testable recommendations. The user expects nine stocks, while the current API reports eight; record the actual membership rather than silently inventing a ninth.

For each fresh breakout, start with the user's fast diagnostic: measure the percentage move from the causal floor to the breakout close and question unusually extended 7–15%+ runs. Then decompose that move into (a) legitimate travel from floor to resistance, (b) the fixed ATR buffer, and (c) avoidable delay between the first meaningful close above a stable ceiling and the engine's signal. Record floor-to-breakout percent and floor-ATR multiples, sessions/price lost to ceiling replacements, whether each replacement came from a genuinely separate range or only a rejected wick, and remaining room to major overhead. A large floor-to-breakout move is a warning to investigate, not automatically an algorithm defect.

## Audit result — data through 18 Aug 2026

The API currently returns eight Core members: NETWEB, CGPOWER, GVT&D, HFCL, MTARTECH, POLYCAB, STLTECH, and KRN. Across the six-month window there are 43 Fresh Breakouts. Average floor-to-breakout extension is 15.6%; 19 signals exceed 15% and 12 exceed 20%. MTARTECH and STLTECH are the clearest extension clusters, both averaging about 20% from the causal floor. This is primarily an execution/chase concern rather than proof that the structural label is wrong.

The stale-anchor-ATR concern is valid in principle but is not the dominant observed problem here. At actual Fresh Breakouts, replacing anchor ATR with breakout-day ATR would usually move the line by less than 0.45% of price. POLYCAB is a useful counterexample: current-ATR tightening could have signalled the weak 13 Aug rejection before price fell back, while the fixed line waited for the stronger 17 Aug close.

The more material issue is failed intraday breaches that create a higher ceiling and then receive a fresh 0.50 ATR buffer. Twenty-one such line raises appear in this sample. Some were protective—especially CGPOWER and KRN, where an early crossing was followed by failure—but some caused material recognition lag: GVT&D in June, STLTECH from 27 Jul to 5 Aug, and NETWEB from 14 Aug onward. This should be modeled with two explicit levels: retain the original/base line as a reclaim/watch threshold, store the highest failed-attempt wick as the strict confirmation cap, and never add another full 0.50 ATR to that wick. The two-line model must be tested against both the late and protective cases before changing `FRESH_BREAKOUT`.

Additional gaps found: circuit-locked zero-range candles can produce structurally correct but non-executable Fresh Breakouts (MTARTECH 4 Aug and STLTECH 25 May); test counts are inherited when overlapping ceilings merge and therefore can overstate tests of the current anchor; near-identical active and major ceilings can both remain visible (NETWEB); and repeated `EARLY_BREAKOUT` events during an existing continuation can be semantically noisy. Persist floor date/price and floor-to-breakout percent/ATR in breakout evidence so the user's diagnostic is visible without manual replay.

Per-stock verdict: NETWEB exposes current moving-goalpost lag; CGPOWER is the key counterexample showing why the old line alone is too permissive; GVT&D has a strong June late-confirmation case; HFCL's local/major handling is broadly sound; MTARTECH's main risk is extreme extension plus circuit executability; POLYCAB supports retaining a stable noise buffer; STLTECH combines goalpost lag with circuit locks; KRN validates the newer compact-local-ceiling behavior and shows protective rejection handling.

## Implemented correction — 18 Aug 2026

The engine now preserves two separate lines after a failed breach. The original ATR-adjusted ceiling remains the base reclaim/watch line, while the failed candle's high becomes the exact strict confirmation cap. A later close above the base but below the cap is `CEILING_RECLAIM`; a high above the cap with a close below it is `FAILED_BREAKOUT`; only the first close above the strict cap is `FRESH_BREAKOUT`. A failed wick never receives another ATR buffer.

After an official breakout, a subsequently confirmed higher swing is treated as `POST_BREAKOUT_SWING`. Its high is the exact next cap, again without an added ATR buffer. Test counts remain attached to their own ceiling lineage, and a Major ceiling is removed when it duplicates or sits below the active line. Fresh-breakout evidence now includes the causal floor date, floor price, floor-to-breakout percent/ATR, and a locked-range warning. Backtest entry skips zero-range sessions because their next-open fill cannot be inferred reliably.

The corrected six-month Core replay produces 51 Fresh Breakouts versus 43 before the correction. This increase is expected because valid nearer post-breakout swings are recognized sooner; it makes Buy Review and the manual execution checklist more important, not less. Regression anchors include STLTECH: 27 Jul swing high ₹592.35 becomes the exact cap and 4 Aug close ₹604.45 is Fresh; NETWEB: the 14 Aug post-breakout swing becomes the exact ₹5,269 cap and the next qualifying close clears it without a duplicate lower Major ceiling.
