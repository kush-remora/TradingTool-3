# Daily Potential Phase D Intake Funnel

This document outlines the first step of the revised Phase D Pipeline: **The Trigger Intake**. 
Instead of endlessly scanning thousands of stocks for quiet accumulation (which is computationally heavy and noisy), the system relies on a **backward-sweep**. It casts a wide, three-pronged net to catch active institutional footprints today, and *only then* looks backward to see if they came from a valid accumulation base.

## The Master Universe: `index_constituents`

Before any triggers are run, the system restricts its vision exclusively to a pre-defined universe of high-quality stocks. This universe is ingested daily and stored in the local `index_constituents` table (where `index_key` is the index name). By strictly limiting the sweep to the Nifty 50, Nifty 100, Midcap 150, Smallcap 250, Microcap 250, and curated sector leaders, the system naturally eliminates untradable penny stocks, SME listings, and operator-driven junk.

## Step 1: The Three-Pronged Intake Architecture

To ensure no institutional footprint is missed, the daily master list is generated by unioning three completely independent sources:

### 1. The Groww Volume Shockers
*   **What it is:** The top 100 stocks across the entire market exhibiting maximum volume shock.
*   **Why we need it:** It provides an immediate, macro-level filter of where the rawest institutional volume is flowing.

### 2. The Chartink Phase D Screener (Loud Footprints)
*   **What it is:** The custom scanner designed to catch Wyckoff Ignition.
*   **Important Context (Chartink Upload):** The CSV/Excel export we upload from Chartink will not just have today's trigger data, but will also include data for the past 9 months.
*   **Why we need it:** It mathematically filters for specific price action combined with volume surges, and provides historical context right in the payload.

### 3. The Local DB Query (Stealth Footprints)
*   **What it is:** A native Kotlin database query run directly against the `stock_delivery_daily` table, comparing Today vs Yesterday.
*   **Why we need it:** Chartink often misses stealth accumulation where the price barely moves but institutions quietly absorb massive supply.
*   **The Math (Two Triggers):** A stock is flagged if it hits *either* of these exact day-over-day conditions:
    1.  **The Absolute Shock:** `Today's Delivery Qty >= (Yesterday's Delivery Qty * 3)`
    2.  **The Absorption Shock:** `Today's Delivery Percentage >= 80%` AND `Today's Delivery Percentage >= (Yesterday's Delivery Percentage + 25%)`. (e.g., Yesterday was 55%, today is 80%. This catches silent institutional hoarding when total volume does not jump).

## Step 1.5: The "Garbage Clearing" Gauntlet

A volume shock does not guarantee Accumulation; it frequently happens during Buying Climaxes, Distribution, or Selling Climaxes. Before any deep 60-day Wyckoff accumulation math is run, the unioned intake list must survive these 3 brutal structural filters to remove the "garbage."

### Rule 1: The Extension Filter (Kills Late-Stage FOMO)
*   **Logic:** If a stock has been grinding up for weeks, a volume shock today is likely a Buying Climax. However, if the stock was completely flat yesterday, and TODAY it explodes 15%, that is a true Phase D start. Therefore, we measure if the stock was overextended *before* today's shock.
*   **Math:** **Yesterday's Close** must be within a specific percentage of **Yesterday's 50 DMA**:
    *   **Large Cap:** Yesterday's Close `<= 10%` above 50 DMA
    *   **Mid Cap:** Yesterday's Close `<= 15%` above 50 DMA
    *   **Small/Micro Cap:** Yesterday's Close `<= 20%` above 50 DMA

### Rule 2: The Green Check (Kills Explicit Distribution)
*   **Logic:** If the volume shock happened on a brutal down day, it is institutional dumping.
*   **Math:** `Today's Close >= Today's Open`.

### Rule 3: The No-Rejection Check (Kills Hidden Distribution / Wicks)
*   **Logic:** A stock can open green, shoot up, and face brutal selling all afternoon, leaving a massive top wick (Shooting Star).
*   **Math:** `Today's Close` must reside in the **Top 25%** of the day's total price range (`High - Low`).

## The Union and Deduplication Process

Once the stocks survive the 5-rule gauntlet, the remaining high-quality candidates undergo:
1. **The Union:** The surviving lists are merged into a single daily candidate pool.
2. **Historical Deduplication / Tracking:** The Kotlin backend checks this unioned list against recent history to see if the stock has triggered repeatedly over the last week (building momentum metadata).

## Next Steps in the Pipeline
This highly curated, structurally sound list is then passed to Step 2: The Backward Sweep. Here, Kotlin analyzes their historical 60-day structures and applies Delivery Conviction ranking to prove if the ignition came from a valid Phase B base.
