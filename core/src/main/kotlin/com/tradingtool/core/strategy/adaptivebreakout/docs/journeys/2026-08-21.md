# Adaptive Breakout Quality Experiment

## Why

The previous breakout-day quality checklist did not improve the backtest. This experiment replaces it with two transparent, independently testable controls: base quality and breakout-day move.

## Implemented

- Added `BASE_QUALITY` as the only selectable quality gate. Breakout-day move remains a diagnostic rule and is not a gate.
- Base quality searches the last 120 pre-breakout sessions for the longest trailing range; PASS requires at least 40 sessions and at most 20% width, WAIT requires at least 20 sessions and at most 30% width.
- Breakout-day move uses close-to-close change from the previous close: PASS below 3%, WAIT from 3% through 6%, REJECT above 6%.
- Added 5-, 10-, and 20-session forward continuation metrics, including positive-close rate and target reachability.
- Delivery remains context only and is never scored.
- Added quality-filter selection and filtered-count display to the backtest page.

## Key correction

The first implementation treated the adaptive engine's candidate-floor date as the base start. Replay inspection showed this is a recent swing anchor, so bases appeared as only 1–9 sessions. The implementation was corrected to search the pre-breakout trailing range before using the result.

## Validation

Focused Kotlin tests, service packaging, frontend page tests, and the frontend production build passed.

Core watchlist replay, 2026-02-21 through 2026-08-21, eight stocks:

| Baseline | 40 | 0 | 6 | 23 / 17 | 57.5% | +0.33% | 1.14 |

The base PASS result is promising but too small to validate. The move diagnostic did not improve this sample, so it is deliberately not available as a quality gate. The baseline forward positive-close rates at 5/10/20 sessions were 55.0% / 60.5% / 61.1%; +5% target reachability was 57.5% / 65.8% / 77.8%.

## Follow-ups

- Keep both controls in experiment mode until a longer out-of-sample sample is available.
- Do not promote the breakout-day move rule to a hard gate based on this six-month core result.
- Investigate why the long-base PASS group is so sparse before loosening thresholds.

## Volume footprint diagnostic

Added a separate, non-gating diagnostic for a high-volume footprint during the prior 20 sessions. It records a qualifying 5×-median-volume event at a relatively cheap price, checks that its low survives, and flags later high-volume weak/rejection candles. It is exposed on each backtest trade but does not alter entry selection.

The 24-month `high_quality` replay produced 353 signals: 5 clean footprints with 4 wins (80.0%), 13 footprints with a later warning with 3 wins from 11 entries (27.3%), and 333 signals without a footprint with 141 wins from 304 entries (46.4%). This is promising but too small for a gate; the next step is more history and out-of-sample validation.

## Trend-alignment diagnostic

The proposed trend-alignment gate was tested as a diagnostic using NIFTY 50 daily candles (instrument token 256265) and point-in-time stock data. A market was considered aligned when NIFTY was above a rising 200-day average. A stock was considered aligned when it was above a rising 200-day average and had positive relative strength versus NIFTY over both 3 and 6 months. The combined condition required both.

The result rejected the proposed gate for this strategy. Over 24 months, the combined aligned group had 73 completed trades, a 37.0% win rate, -1.79% expectancy, and 0.49 profit factor. The unaligned group had 235 trades, a 49.8% win rate, -0.57% expectancy, and 0.80 profit factor. Market alignment alone was also worse (39.5% vs 54.3% when not aligned). The combined aligned result was 30.3% over 12 months and 0% over the latest 6-month window, although the latest window had only one completed aligned trade.

The diagnostic is therefore exposed in each backtest row but is not an active filter. This suggests the current fresh-breakout setup may work earlier in a transition or base-building phase rather than after a stock has already become a strong 200-day trend leader. Any next gate should be tested separately rather than combining this condition with more filters.

## Next-session dip diagnostic

The backtest now records the next trading session's open, low, close, low versus breakout close, low versus breakout-day low, low versus next-session open, and breakout-day close-to-close movement. This is diagnostic data only; the next session's completed low must not be used as a hindsight entry price.

The 24-month `high_quality` replay produced 316 completed trades. When grouped by next-session low versus breakout close, the results were 72.1% wins and +1.70% expectancy when the low stayed above the breakout close; 50.0% and -0.51% for a 0%–1% dip; 43.6% and -1.18% for a 1%–2.5% dip; and 27.8% and -2.82% for a dip greater than 2.5%. The same deterioration appeared when grouping the low versus the next-session open.

This supports a shallow-retest/acceptance hypothesis, not a completed-low quality gate. The next causal test should compare the current next-open entry with waiting for next-session acceptance or a breakout-close limit order, with fills and timing modeled explicitly.

## Stock-wise exploratory review

The backtest page now defaults to a 6-month history and allows the history length to be changed. Watchlist responses already carried one report per stock, so the frontend now exposes those reports in a default Stock-wise results tab. Rows are sortable by the key outcome fields and expand into the stock's complete trade-by-trade replay. The pooled All trades tab remains available for chronological review.

This is intentionally an analysis workflow rather than another gate: the user will inspect individual failures by symbol and date, propose visual patterns, and those hypotheses can then be validated with separate causal tests.

## Backtest control simplification

Removed the Cost RT and Slippage RT inputs from the backtest screen to keep the workflow focused on target, quality, stop loss, history, and entry type. The backend defaults remain unchanged so existing result comparisons continue to use the same return assumptions; these execution assumptions are simply no longer user-editable or shown in the screen guidance.

## Six-month prepared-watchlist replay

The 23-stock `high_quality` watchlist was replayed from 2026-02-21 through 2026-08-21. With no quality filter, the run produced 100 signals, 87 entered trades, 44 targets, 39 stops, 53.0% win rate, -0.20% expectancy, and 0.92 profit factor. With the existing base-quality gate, 56 signals were filtered, leaving 40 entered trades plus 2 gap skips; the result was 22 targets, 15 stops, 59.5% win rate, +0.39% expectancy, and 1.17 profit factor.

The six-month momentum-selected universe is better than the earlier 24-month pooled sample, and the base-quality gate improved the result in this window. The sample is still too small and selective for a 70% conclusion. Volume and trend diagnostics were especially sparse, so they remain review aids rather than active filters.

The Adaptive Breakout scanner watchlist selector now supports multiple selections. Selected watchlists scan concurrently, duplicate symbols are merged into one result row, and single-watchlist/stock scans keep their existing behavior.

The Adaptive Breakout Backtest trade-result table was simplified by removing the Quality, Trend aligned, Next low vs BO close, and Volume footprint columns. Those diagnostics remain available in the response and expanded trade data; the primary table now stays focused on entry, exit, and return outcomes.

## Breakout Buy Review raw-data redesign

Redesigned the Breakout Buy Review screen as a compact inspection view. It now shows the selected breakout day's open, high, low, close, volume, volume versus the prior 10-session average, volume versus the quiet average of the five lowest volumes in the prior 20 sessions, volume change versus the prior 20-session high, Low→High movement, and Open→Close movement. It also shows a small table of prior-20-session days at least 5× the quiet baseline, newest first, with each day's OHLC, volume, quiet-baseline comparison, close position between Low and High, and candle movements. Quality decisions, delivery context, chart interpretation, execution controls, and the calculator were removed from this screen so visual review is based on raw candle evidence.

Added `twentyDayQuietAverageVolume`, `volumeVsTenDayAveragePct`, `volumeVsTwentyDayQuietAveragePct`, `volumeVsPriorTwentyDayHighChangePct`, and `recentAbnormalVolumeDays` to the review response. The 10-day average uses the previous 10 completed sessions; the quiet baseline, prior-high comparison, and abnormal-day table use the previous 20 completed sessions. All baselines exclude the breakout day. The five-times threshold is a display selection only, not a quality gate. The change is limited to Breakout Buy Review; scanner and backtest volume calculations remain unchanged.
